# skincare-hygiene/cleansing-frequency-no-evidence-010

There is no sourced answer to how often you should wash your face, and this row
exists so that fact is retrievable instead of being papered over at answer time.

Searches run on 2026-09-02 looked for randomised evidence on face-washing
frequency, including twice-daily cleansing against acne outcomes, and for bathing
or showering frequency against skin-barrier outcomes in healthy adults. Both
returned nothing eligible. The nearest well-conducted adjacent synthesis, a
Cochrane review of topical acne actives across 49 trials, addresses which
substances to apply and contains no washing-frequency comparison at all.

So every familiar instruction in this area is carried here as unsourced. Wash
twice a day. Cleanse twice at night. Never use hot water. Do not over-wash. None
of them has a primary behind it in this warehouse, and none of them may be spoken
as evidence-based.

Two things this row is careful not to say.

It does not say cleansing frequency is unimportant. Nothing was measured; that is
the whole point. A searched absence and a demonstrated null are different
findings and swapping one for the other just replaces an unsourced claim with an
unsourced claim pointing the other way.

And it does not license importing the hand-washing effect size from elsewhere in
this domain. That number is real, but it is measured on diarrhoeal illness, not
on skin condition. Washing hands to avoid infection and washing a face to change
its state are separate questions with separate evidence, and the one that has a
number is not the one being asked here.

What would close this: a randomised or crossover trial comparing defined washing
frequencies against an objective barrier or lesion endpoint in healthy adults, or
a systematic review pooling such trials. A single-product cosmetic-journal trial
would not close it. A textbook assertion would move the claim from unsourced to
practitioner consensus, which is still near the bottom.
