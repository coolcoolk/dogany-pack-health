# task-management/goal-setting-side-effects-contested-014

"Set specific, difficult goals" is the most transmitted prescription in this
whole domain, and whether it carries systematic costs is genuinely disputed in
the primary literature.

One group of researchers argued in a management journal that the benefits have
been overstated and the harms ignored, listing six side effects: attention
narrowing onto the goal and away from everything else, distorted risk-taking,
more unethical behaviour, less learning, damaged culture, and reduced intrinsic
motivation. The authors of goal-setting theory replied in the same issue, and
their reply is not a partial concession -- it is an accusation that the critique
misuses evidence, cites unrepresentatively and misreports results. The critics
then replied again.

This item does not adjudicate that. It records that the dispute exists, is
published, is between named principals, and is not resolved.

What follows operationally, which does not depend on who is right:

- Do not speak goal-setting prescriptions as settled science. The people who
  built the theory and the people who attacked it are both in print in the same
  journal issue.
- The specific hazard worth watching in a personal system is the one both sides
  agree is a real mechanism even while disagreeing about its size: a goal
  focuses attention on the measured thing and away from the unmeasured thing.
  In a task store that means a stated target quietly reweights what gets
  entered, not just what gets done. That connects directly to
  task-management/target-corruption-completion-metrics-010.

No effect size is stated here because none was verified in this pass. If a
number is ever needed for this, the full texts have to be read first.
