# home/cleaning-agents-lung-function-009

This is a genuine finding wrapped in a false one, and separating them is most of
the value.

The genuine finding: in a European cohort of 6235 people followed for twenty
years with repeated spirometry, women who did the cleaning at home lost lung
function faster than women who did not. FEV1 declined at 22.1 ml per year
against 18.5 in the comparison group. Occupational cleaners came in at 22.4.
FVC declined faster in both groups too. In men, none of it reached significance.

The false one: that this is equivalent to smoking twenty cigarettes a day.

That comparison is attached to this study almost everywhere it is mentioned,
including on the publishing university's own news page. It is not in the paper.
The paper compares cleaners to non-cleaners and reports millilitres per year. It
has no smoking arm and performs no cigarette-equivalence calculation. Somebody
produced the comparison to make the result legible to a general audience, and it
then travelled on its own as though it had been measured. It has not been.

What the actual numbers support is a small effect. About 3.6 millilitres per
year of extra FEV1 decline in home cleaners. Over twenty years that is a real
divergence and it is worth knowing about. It is not a catastrophe and should not
be narrated as one.

Three things keep this at the cautious end of the ladder.

Exposure was self-reported cleaning activity. Nobody measured what anyone
actually breathed. No product, ingredient or concentration is identified, which
means nothing here tells you to switch brands. The read-across that does survive
is about how you apply things rather than what you apply: sprays aerosolise,
small closed rooms concentrate. That is inference from the exposure route, not a
tested comparison.

The effect appeared in women and not in men, in the same cohort under the same
analysis, and the study does not explain why. An unexplained sex-specific
association should make you less sure of the causal story, not more excited
about it.

And nothing here was replicated in Korea. Formulations differ, housing
ventilation differs, and the division of household labour differs -- which
matters a great deal here given that the effect showed up in the group doing the
work. Item 013 has the measured Korean split.

The acute end of the same exposure -- what happens when cleaning chemicals are
combined -- is item 008, and that one is not modest at all.
