# leisure/flow-effortful-leisure-thin-base-008

The idea that demanding leisure is better for you than passive leisure is one of
the most attractive things anyone could tell a person about their free time. It
is also, on the evidence located here, not established.

Its usual root is a study from 1989. Seventy-eight working adults carried pagers
for one week and answered questions when signalled. The reported results were
that the quality of someone's experience tracked whether they were in a state of
matched high challenge and high skill more than it tracked whether they were at
work or at leisure, that most such episodes were reported at work, and that
people nonetheless said they would rather be doing what they were doing during
leisure. The last part is the famous paradox.

One small non-randomised study, from a field where fewer than two thirds of
findings replicate and surviving effects shrink by about a third. That is the
whole base. No replication was found in this pass and no pooled effect size for
flow was found either.

The specific percentages that get quoted for how often people hit flow at work
versus at leisure are everywhere in secondary writing about this study. None of
them could be verified against the article, so none of them are carried here.
A number that lives only in retellings does not ship.

There is also a problem that a bigger study would not fix. Flow is measured by
asking someone how challenged and how skilled they feel; the outcome is measured
by asking the same person, in the same moment, how good they feel. Some of the
relationship between those is the questionnaire talking to itself. And people are
at work during the hours they are sharpest and at leisure when they are tired, so
the work-versus-leisure comparison carries the time of day inside it.

So this is recorded as an absence rather than a finding. Nothing here licenses
telling anyone that their guitar practice counts for more than their evening in
front of the television. It might. Nobody has shown it.

What can still be said is modest and worth keeping. If a person reports being
absorbed in something, that is a real observation about that person on that
occasion, and reflecting it back is fine. It is not evidence about the category
of activity, and it does not rank one person's evening against another's.
