claim: >
  The unit of study work is an ATTEMPT FOLLOWED BY A CHECK, not an exposure.
  Kornell, Hays & Bjork (2009) equated total study time and compared a
  read-only condition (question and answer shown together) against a test
  condition (question alone, participant tries to answer, then the answer is
  revealed) on material constructed so the attempt would FAIL. The failed
  attempt still won: final cued recall 0.41 versus 0.31, d = 0.58 in
  Experiment 1, and the same direction across six experiments. The craft
  inference, stated as an inference: if you look at the answer before genuinely
  attempting it, you have not done an easy rep, you have run the CONTROL
  condition -- the one that measured worse at equal time. Two failure signals
  are therefore legible in a study session: (1) reps where no attempt was made
  before the reveal, and (2) items that fail repeatedly across many sessions
  (handled in learning-habits/reformulate-do-not-grind-017). A third signal is
  explicitly REFUSED: how smooth the session felt. The grading rule that
  survives is the one the tooling states -- "if it counts as a fail in a
  real-life context outside of Anki, then it counts as a fail in Anki as well".
reasoning: >
  The experiment is a clean and directly relevant piece of evidence, and its
  design detail is what makes it usable. The authors solved the item-selection
  problem by using FICTIONAL trivia questions that nobody could have known, so
  every attempt in the test condition was necessarily a failure, and by
  discarding the rare trials where subjects guessed a weak associate correctly.
  In Experiment 2 the total time in the two conditions was equalised, so the
  benefit is not simply more seconds on the material. Crucially the answer was
  REVEALED after the failed attempt in every case: the finding is about
  attempt-then-feedback, and this item never claims that struggling without
  ever seeing the answer helps.
  .
  What is inference and not measurement. The paper compares two experimenter-
  imposed conditions; it does not observe self-directed learners skipping the
  attempt, and it does not measure how often that happens. The claim that a
  premature reveal converts a review into the read-only condition follows from
  the design rather than from a measurement of real study behaviour, and is
  marked as such. Limits of the measured leg: undergraduate participants,
  laboratory setting, trivia facts and weak word associates, delays of five
  minutes in most experiments and 38 hours in one.
  .
  The practitioner half is weaker still and is labelled. The four-way grading
  scheme and its published expectation that the middle grade is used "about
  80-95% of the time" is a tool convention with no study behind it. It is
  carried here only as a coarse session-health reading -- an unusually low
  correct rate means the intake or the material is wrong, not that the person
  is failing -- and never as a target to optimise. Not marked contested: the
  underlying experimental finding is not disputed, and the inference is
  labelled rather than asserted.

# learning-habits/rep-is-attempt-then-check-016

In the gym a failed rep is unmistakable. The bar stops. In study almost nothing
stops, which is why people can put in an hour and have no idea whether anything
happened. Making failure legible starts with defining the rep.

The rep is an attempt followed by a check. Not a reading, not an exposure, not
"going over it". The distinction has been measured under conditions designed so
the attempt could not possibly succeed: participants saw invented trivia
questions with no real answers, tried to answer them, failed, and were then
shown the answer. Against a control group that saw question and answer together
for the same total time, the group that failed first remembered more on the
final test -- 41 percent against 31 percent in the first experiment, with the
same direction holding across six. Failing at a retrieval and then seeing the
answer beat never attempting it.

Turn that around and the first failure signal appears. If you read the front of
a card, feel a flicker of not-quite-knowing, and flip it, you have not performed
an easy rep. You have performed the control condition -- the one that measured
worse at equal time. Nothing was loaded. This is an inference from the study's
design rather than an observation of real learners, and it should be spoken
that way, but it is the sharpest available definition of a wasted rep: the
reveal came before the attempt.

The second signal is an item that keeps failing across many sessions. That one
has its own item in this lane, because the correct response to it is not to try
harder.

The third candidate signal is the one to refuse, and this is where craft
practice and this domain's evidence lane disagree outright. The ordinary study
instinct is to keep working material until it feels smooth, and to treat that
smoothness as the signal that the session went well. This domain's evidence
lane has already ruled on the techniques that produce exactly that feeling:
rereading and highlighting are rated low utility, popular precisely because
they feel productive, weak on durable learning. A craft rule that grades a
session by how fluent it felt is therefore not a neutral heuristic sitting
alongside the evidence -- it is reaching for a signal the evidence lane has
already discredited. Anything in this lane that assumes otherwise is wrong, and
that includes the very common habit of marking an item as merely difficult
whenever recall was slow but eventually arrived.

The grading rule that does survive is blunt and comes from the tooling rather
than from a study: if it would count as a failure outside the session, it counts
as a failure inside it. Partial credit is where honest self-assessment goes to
die.

One coarse instrument is worth keeping and worth not over-reading. The
convention in spaced-repetition practice is that the ordinary "correct with some
effort" grade should account for the large majority of reps -- the published
expectation is roughly 80 to 95 percent. There is no measurement behind that
band. Used as a target it is meaningless. Used as a smoke alarm it is fine: a
sustained correct rate far below it means the intake rate is too high or the
material is badly formed, which are both fixable, and neither of which is a
statement about the person.
