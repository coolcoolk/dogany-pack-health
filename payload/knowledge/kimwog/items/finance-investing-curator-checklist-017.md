# finance-investing/curator-checklist-017

This is the operating checklist for grading anything new in this lane,
distilled from the worked examples above rather than being a finding in its
own right.

Where the ceiling sits. The top of the ladder is reserved for things that
cannot decay: arithmetic identities that hold over the whole market by
construction, and distributional facts measured across the full population of
listed stocks and confirmed outside the country they were first measured in.
Below that sits the large-sample flagship-journal result with no live
rebuttal, together with codified statute and regulator facts -- and those
must always carry both a jurisdiction and the date they were verified.
Single-dataset behavioural findings, simulation outputs, and anything whose
primary source profits from the conclusion sit a further notch down.
Practitioner consensus is discounted harder here than elsewhere, because the
practitioner tier in this domain has a measured misconduct base rate and is
usually paid by the recommendation. Anecdote, guru claims, and backtests are
at the bottom.

Triggers that force a downgrade or a contested flag:

- a performance number with no disclosure of how many strategy variants were
  tried to produce it
- a rule that was chosen after looking at the data it is justified by
- a published predictor or factor, which arrives already carrying an
  unresolved replication dispute and a documented post-publication decline
- a source with a commercial interest in the conclusion it reports
- a statistic quoted in one direction when the underlying source reports both
  directions
- a heuristic fitted on US history alone but presented as universal
- a jurisdiction-bound number with no verification date attached
- an investor-behaviour shortfall figure produced by comparing dollar-weighted
  with time-weighted returns without controlling for when contributions were
  made
- survivorship, at the level of the fund, the index, or the country

What makes this lane different from the clinical ones: randomised trials are
mostly unavailable for return questions, so the ceiling is set by identities
and population-level distributions rather than by trial design; the
practitioner tier has an adverse incentive rather than merely weak evidence;
and findings here decay after they are published, which is a failure mode no
other lane in this warehouse has to model.
