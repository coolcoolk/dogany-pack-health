# finance-investing/adviser-misconduct-base-rate-013

Across the full population of registered US financial advisers, 7 percent
carry a misconduct record, and at some of the largest firms that share is
above 15 percent. About a third of those with a record are repeat offenders,
and having a prior record makes new misconduct roughly five times more likely
than average.

This matters for how advice is weighed, not just for choosing an adviser. In
most domains, practitioner consensus is treated as weak-but-honest evidence:
people who do the work every day, without the study design to prove it. In
retail finance that assumption does not hold, because the practitioner tier
has a measured and non-trivial rate of acting against client interest, and
because the same person is usually compensated by the recommendation being
made.

So in this lane, practitioner consensus carries an extra discount that it
would not carry in a clinical or craft domain, and any recommendation should
be checked against who is paid when it is followed.

Scope: this is a US measurement under a US disclosure regime. It has not been
verified for Korea, and it is not assumed to transfer.
