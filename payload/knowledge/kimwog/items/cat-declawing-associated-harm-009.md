# cat/declawing-associated-harm-009

Declawing is described in a lot of material as a nail procedure, somewhere near
trimming. It is the amputation of the last bone of each toe. That is not a
finding from a study; it is what the operation is. The study supplies what
follows.

A comparative cohort matched a hundred and thirty-seven declawed cats against the
same number of non-declawed ones and found the declawed group at higher odds of
back pain, of urinating and defecating outside the box, of biting, and of
over-grooming. The elimination result was the largest of them by a distance.
Radiographs found retained fragments of bone in nearly two thirds of the declawed
cats.

The first thing anyone says about a study like that is that the causation might
run backwards -- that cats who were already biting or soiling were the ones
people chose to declaw. It is a fair objection to the headline comparison. It is
much harder to make against the comparison inside the declawed group, where the
cats with bone fragments left behind fared worse than the ones without. Nothing
about a household's decision to declaw predicts whether the surgeon left bone in
the toe. The study also reports that even where technique looked clean, the
declawed cats were still at higher odds of biting and of eliminating outside the
box.

It is still one retrospective cohort. The outcomes were assessed rather than
measured, the pairs include shelter cats whose histories are often unknown, and
nobody randomised anything. So the correct sentence is that declawed cats in this
cohort did worse on four measures and that reverse causation is an awkward
explanation for the fragment result -- not that declawing was proven to cause
them.

One more thing belongs alongside this. "The cat seems fine" is a weak observation
in this species. Back pain at roughly three times the odds is not something read
off a cat's demeanour, and the whole reason a validated feline pain scale exists
is that the signal is subtle enough to need training. Absence of visible distress
is not evidence here.

Finally, something searched and not found. Whether elective declawing is
prohibited in Korea could not be established from primary legal sources in this
pass. The prohibition on injuring an animal carries exceptions for prevention and
treatment, and nothing located resolves whether an elective claw removal sits
inside them. It should not be stated as banned, and it should not be stated as
permitted.
