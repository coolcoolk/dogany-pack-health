# work-career/job-change-wage-growth-005

A meaningful share of early-career pay growth arrives at the moment of
changing employer, not gradually inside one.

The classic estimate, from United States longitudinal data: in the first ten
years of working life a typical worker holds seven jobs -- roughly two thirds
of all the jobs he will ever hold -- and pay gains at job changes account for
at least a third of early-career wage growth. Pay is also the main thing
driving the decision to move.

The framing in the source is worth keeping. All that job-changing is not
instability to be fixed; it is the search process by which someone converges
on a match that lasts.

Now the limits, which matter more than usual here because this is the finding
most likely to be turned into a rule of thumb.

The sample is young men. The country is the United States. The data is from
before essentially every structural change in hiring that has happened since.
So the direction is what to carry -- switching is where a real chunk of early
pay growth happens -- and the specific fraction is not a current parameter.

It should especially not be carried into the Korean labour market unchanged.
Korean pay is much more strongly indexed to tenure with one employer, which
mechanically changes the arithmetic of leaving. That is a separate item.
