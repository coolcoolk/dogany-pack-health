# exercise/volume-landmarks-heuristic-015

The idea of ramping weekly sets from a minimum-effective floor up toward a
maximum-recoverable ceiling across a training block, then deloading, is a
sound and widely used planning scaffold. The specific numbers attached to
that scaffold in popular tables (a particular set count for "chest MEV",
another for "chest MRV", and so on) come from coaching experience and
iteration, not from controlled trials -- they are useful starting defaults,
not measured facts.

Numbers broken down further, to the level of a sub-part of a muscle (say, a
separate landmark for upper chest versus lower chest), have even less behind
them: published landmarks exist mainly at the whole-muscle-group level, and
splitting them into subregion numbers is an engineering guess layered on top
of an already-heuristic number. Present any such number honestly as a
starting point to be adjusted by the individual's actual recovery and
progress, never as an established scientific fact.
