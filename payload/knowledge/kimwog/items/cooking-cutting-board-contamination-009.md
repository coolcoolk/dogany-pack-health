# cooking/cutting-board-contamination-009

Cooking chicken to temperature solves one problem. It does nothing about the
problem created before the chicken reached the pan.

When contaminated chicken was chopped under observation, viable Campylobacter
was recovered well beyond the board: up to 60 cm away when the bird carried a low
load, and up to 120 cm away at medium and high loads. The contaminated zone is
not the board. It is roughly an arm's length of counter in every direction.

The more uncomfortable half is what happened to the boards. Across real kitchens,
the cleaning people actually do did not clear the organism. Scraping the board
with a knife brought the load down from 2.97 to 2.10 log per 100 square
centimetres, and left more than half the boards still positive. Rinsing under the
tap left 2.85 log and 60 percent positive. The one kitchen using a disinfectant
got down to 1.6 log and the board still tested positive. Note the sample sizes
before drawing a ranking from that: seventeen kitchens, five, and one. What the
data support is the direction they share, not an ordering. Every method left the
board contaminated.

Campylobacter did not persist indefinitely. It survived longest on plastic --
about four and a half hours at 15C and three and a half at 25C -- against roughly
three hours on stainless steel and wood, which the authors read as a
cross-contamination window of three to four and a half hours. That bound cuts one
way only. It is not permission to leave the board.

The practical reading for a cooking session is two instructions, both cheap. Keep
a board that only ever meets raw poultry, and when the raw work is done, clean
the counter around it, not just the board. Both are worth doing even if this
single study is wrong, which is the standard that justifies acting on one study.

One thing deliberately not shipped here: the famous instruction never to wash raw
chicken. It is real public-health advice, but in this pass it could only be
traced to regulator communications rather than to a retrievable primary
measurement, so it was refused and recorded rather than smuggled in alongside
evidence that belongs to a different claim.
