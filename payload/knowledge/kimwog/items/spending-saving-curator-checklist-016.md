# spending-saving/curator-checklist-016

This is the operating checklist for grading anything new in this lane,
distilled from the worked examples above rather than being a finding in its own
right.

Where the ceiling sits. The top of the ladder is reserved for measurement facts
checked against something outside the respondent: national accounts, industry
aggregates, administrative records, or a randomised experiment on the recording
instrument itself. That placement is not a preference for methodology over
substance. It is because in this domain the instrument is the main source of
error, and no amount of care about the behavioural question rescues a badly
measured input. No behavioural result sits at the top, however large the effect.

Below that: randomised field experiments, population-scale administrative
evidence, meta-analyses of randomised trials that report their publication-bias
diagnostics, and codified statute facts -- the last always carrying a
jurisdiction and the date they were verified. Below that again: laboratory and
scenario studies, single field experiments in one population, quasi-experiments
over people who chose to enrol, and synthesis rules like this one. At the
bottom: personal-finance media, practitioner rules, and trade books.

That last line deserves emphasis, because it is the structural fact about this
domain. In the clinical lanes, the widely repeated advice is usually a distorted
version of something real. Here, the most widely repeated rules are mostly at the
bottom of the ladder outright, with nothing underneath them at all.

Triggers that force a downgrade or a contested flag:

- a rule stated as a fixed ratio or a fixed number of months, with no cited
  measurement behind the number
- a spending or saving claim built on self-report with no external benchmark
- a general behavioural effect quoted as though it had been measured on money
- an effect size taken from an early striking experiment when a later synthesis
  exists
- a laboratory result about hypothetical spending presented as a transaction
  finding
- a tax or product parameter with no verification date, or read from a bank,
  card issuer or brokerage instead of from the statute or the regulator
- a result measured inside an employer or pension system and transferred to a
  self-directed account
- a low-income or developing-economy field experiment transferred to a
  middle-income household
- an intervention bundle whose effect gets attributed to one of its components
- a per-category conclusion drawn from a log without first checking entry
  coverage

What makes this lane different from the investing one next door. Its ceiling is
measurement validation rather than arithmetic identity, because no identity is
available here. Its adverse incentive is different in kind: the practitioner tier
is not mainly conflicted, it is mainly unsourced, so the first trigger fires on a
missing citation rather than on a commercial interest. And uniquely, the data
being reasoned over is produced by the person being advised, which puts the
measuring instrument inside the analysis and is why the last trigger exists at
all -- a log that stops being kept looks exactly like a household that stopped
spending.
