# work-career/founder-age-middle-aged-017

Successful founders are middle-aged. The mean age at founding for the fastest
growing one in a thousand new US ventures is 45.0.

That comes from administrative data covering the full population of firms,
owners and workers -- not from a sample of funded start-ups, and not from press
coverage. Which matters, because the youth story is manufactured by exactly
that selection: a handful of very young founders became famous, and the
denominator was never shown.

The result survives the obvious objections. It holds in high-technology
sectors. It holds in the entrepreneurial hubs. It holds when the outcome is a
successful exit rather than growth.

The second finding is the one to act on. Prior experience in the specific
industry predicts a much higher rate of success. Age may partly be standing
in for that, though the paper does not separate the two causally. So the reading is not "wait until you are 45" -- it is that domain
knowledge is doing the work, and someone entering an industry they have worked
in is playing a different game from someone entering one they have not.

Two things this does not establish. It is descriptive, so it does not prove
that being older causes better outcomes. And it says nothing about the Korean
case, where the age distribution of business founding is shaped by a very
different mechanism -- people leaving a main job in their fifties.
