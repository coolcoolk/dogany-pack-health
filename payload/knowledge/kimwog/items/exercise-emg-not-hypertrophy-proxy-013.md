# exercise/emg-not-hypertrophy-proxy-013

How hard a muscle "lights up" on surface EMG during a single exercise does
not reliably predict how much that muscle will grow over weeks of training,
and it cannot be used to say one exercise stimulates a muscle more than
another. Clear mismatches exist: hip thrusts show more glute EMG than squats
but grow the glutes about the same amount, and very different EMG readings
between heavy and light loads still produce similar growth.

Curator rule this finding carries: any claim in the engine that a specific
exercise biases growth toward a specific muscle region must be graded by what
kind of evidence backs it. Longitudinal MRI or muscle-size evidence is strong
support; acute EMG evidence alone is weak support and should not be presented
with the confidence a longitudinal finding earns.
