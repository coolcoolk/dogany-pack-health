# work-career/unstructured-interview-illusion-003

A free-form conversational interview is not just a weak signal. In a
controlled test it made predictions worse than not interviewing at all.

Participants were asked to predict other students' semester results using
genuinely useful information, such as prior results. Some also got an
unstructured interview. In one condition the person being interviewed was
answering with a random response system, so the answers carried no
information whatsoever.

Three findings, all uncomfortable:

People formed impressions from the random interview just as confidently as
from a real one. We are extremely good at constructing a coherent story out of
whatever we are given.

Adding the interview made predictions less accurate. Not neutral, worse. The
non-diagnostic material crowded out the diagnostic material that was already
on the table.

Asked whether they wanted an interview, participants said yes so strongly
that they preferred a random interview to none.

Watching the interview rather than conducting it barely helped.

The practical reading is narrow and firm: the fix for a bad hiring
conversation is not a better free-form conversation. It is structure -- the
same questions, the same order, scored against the same criteria -- which the
meta-analytic evidence puts at more than twice the validity coefficient (.42
against .19). And when someone reports a strong gut feeling from an interview,
remember that in this test the feeling was just as confident when the answers
were random: its strength says little about how much it knows about the
candidate.
