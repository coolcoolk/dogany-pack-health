# cat/milk-lactase-decline-008

The saucer of milk is the oldest picture in cat keeping, and the standard
correction -- cats are lactose intolerant -- is delivered with more confidence
than the measurement behind it supports. The measurement is more interesting than
either.

One study measured the digestive enzymes in the small intestine of fifty-eight
cats, adults and kittens. Lactase activity in newborns was high and by six weeks
of age it had fallen to roughly a seventh of that. The other enzymes measured --
the ones for starch and for table sugar -- did not change with age at all. So the
decline is specific to milk sugar, and it happens around weaning.

The most useful part is what happened when they varied the diet. Cats were fed
carbohydrate-free, starch-heavy, sucrose-heavy and lactose-heavy diets, and diet
made no difference to enzyme activity. Feeding lactose did not produce more
lactase. That is the direct answer to the argument a household actually makes,
which is that the cat has had milk for years and is fine with it.

Two things the popular correction leaves out.

The same paper records that some adult cats had unusually high lactase activity
in the jejunum. So the blanket claim about any individual cat is not what was
measured. The population declines sharply; a minority apparently does not. An
agent should not tell someone their cat cannot process lactose, because this
source contains cats that seem to.

And nobody in this study gave a cat milk. This is enzyme activity in tissue. The
step from low lactase to an upset stomach is biologically reasonable and it is an
inference, not a finding, and no source found in this sprint measured the
clinical side. Anything about how much causes what is unsupported in both
directions.

The study is also from 1993, from one laboratory, in a journal that PubMed does
not index, and a search for a replication found none. That is a reason to speak
carefully, not a reason to discard it.

What survives all of that is still enough. Milk has no nutritional argument in
its favour for an adult cat, and it has a measured mechanism against it in most
of them. That gets you to "not a good idea" without having to exaggerate on the
way.
