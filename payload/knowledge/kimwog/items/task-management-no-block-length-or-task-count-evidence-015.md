# task-management/no-block-length-or-task-count-evidence-015

There is no established evidence for any of the specific numbers this domain
runs on.

Not supported by any located primary source:

- The 25-minute work interval, or the 5-minute break that goes with it.
- Any particular daily task count, including "three most important tasks".
- Any ceiling on how many items a person should hold open.
- Any urgent/important quadrant prescription.
- Any specific ratio of focused to unfocused time.

What does exist is narrow and points sideways. The one located trial of a
fixed-schedule work pattern against self-chosen breaks ran 24 minutes of work
and 6 of break -- not 25 and 5 -- and compared it against letting people choose,
not against a different interval. It found no difference in effort or task
completion. The one meta-analysis of short breaks states that no standard for
break length is established, and the only break-duration moderator that reached
significance in it favoured LONGER breaks, which is the opposite direction from
the short-interval prescription.

Read the scope precisely, because the opposite over-read is the worse mistake:

- This is an absence of evidence, NOT a demonstrated null. Nobody has shown
  that block length is irrelevant. The comparisons have not been run.
- Structure itself is not being refused. Fixed schedules do beat self-chosen
  timing on concentration, motivation, fatigue and on stopping sessions from
  drifting long -- see
  task-management/breaks-wellbeing-not-performance-012. It is the NUMBER that
  has nothing behind it.

The practical consequence: a specific interval may be proposed as a convention
to remove a decision, never as a research-backed optimum. If asked what the
right block length is, the correct answer is that nobody has measured it, and
the useful follow-up is what the person's own logged sessions actually look
like -- which is a reference class of the right population, per
task-management/reference-class-base-rate-002.
