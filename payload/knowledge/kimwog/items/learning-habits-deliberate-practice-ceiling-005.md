# learning-habits/deliberate-practice-ceiling-005

The popularized "10,000 hours" idea overstates what deliberate practice
actually explains. Across a large meta-analysis (157 effect sizes), practice
accounts for only about 14% of the variance in performance (12% before the
authors' 2018 corrigendum -- use the corrected figure) -- meaning 86% is not
explained by practice. Some of that is measurement error rather than 'other
factors': the headline is not corrected for it. The meta-analysis tests whether
accumulated practice accounts for most of the differences between people; it
does not test the 10,000-hour figure itself.

The underlying evidence is observational/correlational, not experimental, so
even though the estimate is precise, it caps at a correlational-design
confidence tier, not a causal one. It is also disputed on what counts as
deliberate practice: the original proposer's re-analysis of only the studies
that match the original definition reports about 29%. Quote the 14% as what
that meta-analysis found, not as a settled ceiling.
