# home/kr-mandated-ventilation-rate-004

Most people in a modern Korean apartment already own a ventilation system. Quite
a lot of them have never turned it on.

The rule behind that is 건축물의 설비기준 등에 관한 규칙 제11조, currently in
force as of 2026-08-24. Newly built or remodelled housing in the listed
categories -- apartment complexes of thirty or more units among them -- must be
fitted with natural or mechanical ventilation equipment capable of at least half
an air change per hour.

Two corrections belong with that.

The threshold is thirty units. Korean summaries of this provision, including
ones that surface at the top of a search, still say a hundred. The current text
says thirty. This is the same trap the warehouse already recorded for Korean
dietary reference figures: a secondary source that was accurate about a previous
edition and is now quietly wrong.

And half an air change per hour is a design requirement on equipment, not a
statement about your air. It constrains what the builder must install and what
that equipment must be capable of. It says nothing about whether you run it,
whether the filter is choked, whether the supply path is blocked by furniture,
or what your flat actually achieves on a Tuesday in February. A home can be
fully compliant and badly ventilated at the same time.

What this is genuinely useful for is the prior it sets. If the building is a
Korean apartment complex of any size built or substantially remodelled under
current rules, the working assumption should be that a ventilation unit is
installed and that finding and using it is a cheaper first move than buying
anything. That connects to the one household-environment finding that is solidly
evidenced -- damp and mould, item 001 -- because ventilation is the main lever
an occupant has on indoor humidity.

What this item does not claim: that hitting the design rate produces a measured
health benefit. No such demonstration was searched for or found here. The rule
is a rule.
