# work-career/negotiation-context-bound-007

What the evidence supports about negotiating is situational, not personal.

The pooled analysis of 123 effect sizes covering just under 11,000 people
finds a small average gender difference in economic negotiation outcomes,
favouring men, of about 0.20 in standardised terms. That headline is the least
interesting part.

The moderators are bigger than the main effect and they point at things
anyone can change:

- Experience. With no negotiation experience the gap is 0.36. With experience
  it is 0.12.
- Information about the bargaining range. Knowing the range shrinks the gap.
- Negotiating on behalf of someone else. Negotiating for another individual
  drops the gap to about 0.05, against 0.25 for oneself.

Under the combination of conditions with the least role conflict, the gap does
not merely close, it reverses -- the analysis's model predicts a gap of 0.49
at the most role conflict and -0.17 at the least. Those are model endpoints,
not groups anyone measured directly.

So two of the moves are unglamorous and within reach: find out the range
before you talk, and get repetitions. Both are situational, and both are available.
The advice to become a different kind of person is not supported by this
evidence.

One weaker claim, kept separate because its evidence is weaker: the first
number named tends to anchor where the deal lands -- unless the other side
deliberately thinks about your alternatives, your walk-away point or its own
target, which in the original experiments wiped the advantage out. That effect
is well replicated in the laboratory with student participants. It has not been
established for real salary negotiations at the same strength, so it should be
offered as a laboratory-supported heuristic rather than a measured workplace
result.
