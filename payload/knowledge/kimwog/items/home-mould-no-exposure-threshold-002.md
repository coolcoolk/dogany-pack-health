# home/mould-no-exposure-threshold-002

The single most useful sentence in the WHO dampness guideline is a refusal.

> As the relations between dampness, microbial exposure and health effects
> cannot be quantified precisely, no quantitative health-based guideline values
> or thresholds can be recommended for acceptable levels of contamination with
> microorganisms.

Read that alongside item 001 and the shape of the whole area becomes clear.
Damp, mouldy buildings are worse for the people in them -- that part is settled
well enough for a health authority to say so plainly. But there is no number.
No spore count per cubic metre, no species list, no lab report that returns a
verdict. WHO looked at whether one could be set and said it could not.

That has three practical consequences, and they are the reason this is a
separate item rather than a footnote.

An air sample cannot grade your home. If someone measures spores in your air
and tells you the result is acceptable or unacceptable, ask what they are
comparing it against. There is no health-based value to compare against. The
measurement can be real and the interpretation still be invented.

Identifying the species does not change what you do. The recommended action --
find the water, stop the water, remediate the growth -- is the same regardless
of which organism is growing. Species identification can matter in specific
clinical situations, but it does not modify the household instruction.

And any product or service selling a pass/fail verdict against a measured mould
level is selling a threshold the responsible authority declined to set. That
includes testing kits, "certified mould-free" inspections, and air-quality
gadgets that report a mould index.

What replaces the number is cruder and more reliable: is there visible damp, is
there visible growth, is there a building condition producing water where it
should not be. Those are the signals with evidence behind them, and they are
free to check.
