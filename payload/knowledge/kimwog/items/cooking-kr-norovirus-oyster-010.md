# cooking/kr-norovirus-oyster-010

Shucked oysters in Korea are sold in two categories, and the packaging says
which. 생식용 means the product is intended to be eaten raw. 가열조리용, or the
plainer 익혀 먹는, means it is not.

For the second category the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety gives a specific
instruction: heat to a centre temperature of 85C and hold there for at least one
minute. Norovirus is destroyed by heat, and adequate cooking is what prevents the
infection. The advisory is issued for winter, which is when norovirus circulates
in Korea.

Two features of this rule matter for a cooking session.

It is triggered by a label, not by the ingredient. The right first question about
a bag of shucked oysters is what the package says, and a product marked for
cooking must not be quietly turned into a raw dish because the recipe called for
one.

And its number is higher than the general meat table. Korean core-temperature
guidance for cooking tops out at 74C, for poultry and for reheated leftovers. A
virus is not a vegetative bacterium and gets its own, higher target. A session
that has internalised "74C is the safe number" will undercook these oysters.

Two things are left out on purpose. The advisory read here spoke about oysters;
the broader claim that all shellfish take 85C for a minute circulates in Korean
media but was not in the government text, so it is not shipped. And the
year-on-year increase in reported oyster food-poisoning cases that travelled with
this advisory in press coverage was not in the source either, so the seasonality
is carried and the multiplier is not.
