# mental-health/mindfulness-pitfalls-006

Mindfulness research is this domain's cautionary case study. A commonly
repeated "27-31% reduction" statistic is a misreading of the source data --
it's the share of effect-size tests found to be upwardly biased, not an
effect-size shrinkage figure. Positive results are extremely saturated (87% of
RCT abstracts report a positive finding, well above the base rate you'd expect
from true power), and trial preregistration failed to prevent this.

The genuine effect versus a passive control is only small-to-moderate and
shrinks further, sometimes to non-significance, against an active control.
Treat "mindfulness for stress" claims as contested and cap them well below a
top grade.
