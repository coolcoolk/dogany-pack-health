# finance-investing/lump-sum-vs-cost-averaging-010

When a lump of money is already available, putting it to work immediately has
historically beaten feeding it in over several months about two-thirds of the
time. On a global equity index over 1976-2022, measured a year later,
investing at once won 68 percent of the time.

Two qualifications keep this honest. First, the margin is small: for a 60/40
portfolio the average ending values were about 107,453 for a three-month
staged entry and 109,360 for investing at once -- a difference of 1.8
percent. Second, spreading the money in still beat leaving it in cash 69
percent of the time, so the real comparison is rarely "stage it or invest it"
but "invest it or sit on it".

The reason is not that markets can be predicted. It is that cash usually
loses to stocks and bonds over a year, so any period spent in cash is a
period of forgone risk premium.

One thing must be said whenever this is used: the study measuring it was
published by an asset manager, whose business benefits from an invest-now
conclusion. That does not make the result wrong -- an independent theoretical
result from 1979 reaches the same direction -- but it is why this claim is
carried lower on the confidence ladder than its sample size alone would
suggest, and why staged entry remains a legitimate choice for someone whose
alternative is not investing at all.
