# work-career/kr-new-firm-survival-base-rate-015

The Korean base rate, from national administrative records rather than from
anybody's opinion:

Of firms born in 2022, 64.4 percent were still alive one year later. Of firms
born in 2018, 36.4 percent were still alive five years later. For scale, the
same release counts 7.64 million active firms and 922,000 new ones in 2024.

So roughly one in three does not last a year, and only about one in three is
still standing at five.

Two cautions that change how the number should be used.

It is Korean. The figure most often quoted in English-language business
writing describes the United States, where survival is higher -- of US
private-sector establishments born in March 2013, 79.6 percent made it one year
and 50.6 percent five -- and the two are routinely swapped. They are different labour markets, different registration
regimes and different counting units.

And "death" here means the administrative record ended. It does not separate a
business that failed from one the owner closed on purpose, sold, or folded
into something else. It also says nothing about whether the owner ended up
better off. A closed business is not automatically a bad outcome.

One technical point worth knowing before quoting both numbers together: the
one-year and five-year figures are about different birth cohorts, so they
cannot be chained into a survival curve for a single group of firms. And this
release is provisional.
