# skincare-hygiene/self-assessed-outcome-floor-008

Almost every skincare claim anyone brings is a before-and-after with no control
arm. This is what that is worth.

A preregistered model-based meta-analysis pooled 21,627 subjects from randomised,
double-blind, placebo-controlled acne trials and modelled what happened in the
PLACEBO arms. Simulated for a typical population at twelve weeks:

- total lesion counts down by 19.05, interval 16.54 to 21.52
- non-inflammatory lesions down by 10.71
- inflammatory lesions down by 8.58
- investigator-rated treatment success in 11.83 percent, interval 9.41 to 14.35

Nineteen fewer lesions and roughly one in eight rated a success, from nothing.

Two things sharpen that. First, these are COUNTED lesions -- about as objective an
endpoint as this field has. Whatever the movement on a countable outcome is, the
movement on "does my skin look better to me" is larger, not smaller. The
direction of that inference is safe even though the size of it is not derivable
here and must not be invented.

Second, and more useful: the placebo response was not a constant. It varied with
baseline lesion counts, sex composition, ethnic composition, sample size, dosage
form, comparator drug, the background skincare programme imposed on both arms,
number of centres, study location, publication year -- and funding source. When
the apparent response to nothing tracks who paid for the study and what routine
the placebo group was told to follow, an uncontrolled observation is not weak
evidence about the product. It is not evidence about the product at all.

The contrast worth keeping in view is the sunscreen photoaging trial, which
handled this correctly: assessors blinded to allocation graded skin
microtopography, rather than asking participants whether they thought they looked
better. That design choice is the reason its 24 percent figure means something.

One misuse to refuse. This does not say topical treatments do not work. The same
trials do separate active arms from placebo arms. It says that without a control
arm you cannot tell which one you are looking at, and that the gap you are
looking at is usually smaller than the movement placebo alone produces.
