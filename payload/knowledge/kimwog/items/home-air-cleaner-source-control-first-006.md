# home/air-cleaner-source-control-first-006

The most useful thing in the current review literature on cleaning indoor air is
an ordering, and it is not the ordering the market implies.

Policy and source control are described as the most effective approaches.
Engineering interventions -- air cleaners, filtration -- are described as having
received the most research attention. Those are two different superlatives, and
the gap between them is the finding. Effort has concentrated on the device layer
while the leverage sits upstream of it.

That does not make air cleaners useless. The same review reports that home and
school interventions produced improvements in asthma and COPD symptoms.
Combined, multilevel approaches gave mixed results, and the reviewers flag
long-term effectiveness as still poorly understood. A cleaner is a defensible
adjunct in a household with someone who has a respiratory diagnosis, once the
source has been dealt with.

Three boundaries are worth holding firmly.

There are two different claims wearing the same word. A portable cleaner reduces
particle concentration in the room it occupies -- that is a physical measurement
and nobody disputes it here. Whether that produces a measured health improvement
is a separate question with thinner and less consistent evidence. When someone
tells you a purifier works, find out which claim they are making.

The population studied is people with chronic respiratory disease. Most consumer
purifiers are sold to people without any respiratory diagnosis at all. Nothing
in this evidence base speaks to that group, and carrying the finding across is
an extrapolation, not a result.

And there is no effect size here to quote. This is a review with
recommendations, not a meta-analysis. If someone cites a percentage improvement,
they are citing something else, and that something else needs its own design
limits stated before it is repeated.

One structural point, which is why this item cross-references the damp item: a
filter cannot remove a wet wall. When the problem is a source -- moisture, a
combustion appliance, a material off-gassing, a pet, a smoker -- the device
layer is treating a symptom that regenerates as fast as it is removed. That is
what "source control first" means in practice, and it is free.

No manufacturer material was used anywhere in this item. A device maker is not a
source for its own device's efficacy, and that rule was applied here without
exception.
