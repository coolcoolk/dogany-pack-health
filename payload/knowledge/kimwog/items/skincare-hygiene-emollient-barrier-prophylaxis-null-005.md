# skincare-hygiene/emollient-barrier-prophylaxis-null-005

The barrier hypothesis is the mechanism underneath most of what this domain
sells. Support the outermost layer of skin, the story goes, and downstream
trouble does not happen. It has been given a genuinely good test, and it failed.

A Cochrane prospective individual participant data meta-analysis identified 33
randomised trials of skin care interventions in healthy term infants without
pre-existing eczema, food allergy or other skin disease -- 25,827 participants
identified, 11 studies and 5,217 participants entering meta-analyses, most of the
data from emollient trials.

What it found:

- No change in eczema risk by one to three years. Risk ratio 1.03, interval 0.81
  to 1.31, moderate certainty.
- No change in time to onset. Hazard ratio 0.86, interval 0.65 to 1.14, moderate
  certainty.
- More skin infection during the intervention period. Risk ratio 1.33, interval
  1.01 to 1.75, about 17 additional cases per 1,000 infants, moderate certainty.
- A possible increase in IgE-mediated food allergy. Risk ratio 2.53, but the
  interval is 0.99 to 6.49, low certainty, and it comes from one trial of 976
  participants.

Two things make this harder to dismiss than the usual null. First, the effect
does not hide in a subgroup: prespecified analyses by age, intervention
duration, hereditary risk, filaggrin mutation, a specified chromosome 11 variant
and intervention type all came back flat. Filaggrin is the barrier gene. If
barrier support worked anywhere, it should have worked there. Second, the design
is individual participant data pooled prospectively, not a retrospective sweep
of published abstracts.

Grade the two harm signals differently, because they are not the same strength.
The infection finding is moderate certainty across six trials and is safe to
state. The food-allergy finding is one low-certainty trial whose interval touches
no effect; it is the reason the practice stopped being recommended, not a
demonstrated injury, and it should be spoken that way.

One boundary that must not be crossed. This is about PREVENTION in infants who do
not have eczema. It says nothing about emollients for someone who already has
eczema, where they remain standard care. The null belongs to the prophylactic
claim only.
