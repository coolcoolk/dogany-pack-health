# work-career/weak-ties-causal-inverted-u-012

Loose contacts really do move people into jobs. The relationship is not
monotonic, and it flips by industry.

The evidence is unusually strong for a networking claim. Randomised
experiments on a professional platform's connection-recommendation system
varied how many weak ties appeared in people's networks, across more than 20
million people over five years, covering 2 billion new connections and 600,000
new jobs.

Three corrections to the familiar slogan:

The curve bends. Weaker ties help job transmission up to a point, then the
returns to further weakness diminish. It is an inverted U, not a straight
line.

The measurement matters. Measured by shared connections, moderately weak ties
did the most. Measured by how much people actually interacted, the weakest
ties did the most. Those are not the same recommendation.

The industry matters, and it reverses. In more digital industries, weak ties
increased job mobility. In less digital industries, STRONG ties did.

So the practical reading is not "collect acquaintances". It is closer to:
find out which kind of industry you are in, and in a less digital one, depth
of relationship is doing the work that breadth does elsewhere.

Two limits. This is one platform, and a tie there is a specific measurable
object, not the whole of a professional relationship. And the experiments were
run on people using the product, which attracted published criticism about
consent -- worth knowing about how the result was made, though it does not
undermine the estimate.
