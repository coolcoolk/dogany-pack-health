# finance-investing/market-timing-day-symmetry-009

You will see this statistic constantly: miss the ten best days and your
returns collapse, therefore stay invested. The underlying study is real --
roughly 160,000 daily returns across 15 international markets -- and the
downside number checks out: missing the best 10 days left portfolios 50.8
percent less valuable on average.

What almost never gets quoted is the other half of the same table. Avoiding
the worst 10 days left portfolios 150.4 percent MORE valuable. At 100 days the
asymmetry is extreme in the opposite direction from the marketing version:
missing the best 100 days costs 97.7 percent of terminal wealth, avoiding the
worst 100 days multiplies it by a factor of over 265.

So the honest reading is not "the good days matter more than the bad days."
It is that a tiny number of days dominates long-run outcomes in both
directions, which makes successful timing extraordinarily valuable and
correspondingly extraordinarily unlikely -- ten days is under 0.1 percent of
all trading days in the average market.

The conclusion (stay invested) is defensible. The one-sided argument usually
given for it is not, and it should be corrected rather than repeated, because
an investor who accepts the one-sided version has been given a false picture
of what timing would be worth if it worked.
