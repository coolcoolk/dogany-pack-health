# skincare-hygiene/retinoid-evidence-vs-otc-derivatives-007

If one topical active in this domain has real randomised support, it is
tretinoin. The support is also thinner than its reputation, and it does not
belong to the products sold on the strength of it.

A systematic review screened 180 studies and kept 7 randomised controlled trials.
Tretinoin from 0.025 to 5 percent, treatment from 3 months to 24 months. All
seven reported improvement in the clinical appearance of photoaging -- wrinkling,
mottled hyperpigmentation, sallowness, lentigines -- some as early as a month and
sustained out to two years. All reported it as safe and well tolerated.

Then the review's own limitation section, which is the honest part: different
formulations, different outcome measures, few recent studies, no uniformity
across the evidence. No meta-analysis was performed. Seven small heterogeneous
trials pointing the same way is a real signal and a modest one.

Now the substitution that matters more than any of the above. Every one of those
trials used tretinoin, which is a prescription medicine. The wrinkle-improvement
actives a Korean consumer can actually buy in a cosmetic are a different set:
retinol, retinyl palmitate, adenosine, polyethoxylated retinamide, at
concentrations fixed by the regulator. No randomised trial evidence for those
derivatives at those concentrations was located in this pass. Chemical family
resemblance is not efficacy transfer. The retail category rests on the reader not
noticing where the trials stop and the shelf begins.

Calibrate against the neighbouring literature before extending any of this. A
Cochrane review of six commonly sold topical acne actives pooled 49 trials and
3,880 participants and concluded that azelaic acid is probably worse than benzoyl
peroxide and probably no different from tretinoin, and that for essentially every
other comparison the evidence was low or very low quality. Its own closing
recommendation was for more methodologically robust head-to-head trials. That is
where the best-studied topical category ends up after 49 trials. Any less-studied
active with a stronger-sounding claim should be treated as starting further back,
not level.

This item records what the evidence is. It is not a recommendation, it contains
no regimen, and the question of what a particular person should apply to their
face is a clinician's, not a warehouse's.
