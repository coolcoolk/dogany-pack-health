# skincare-hygiene/kr-spf-pa-labelling-012

The numbers on a Korean sunscreen are set by ministry notice, and the rules
behind them break most of the comparisons people make with them.

Two conventions govern the SPF figure.

First, the printed number is allowed to be lower than the measured one. The rule
permits any integer from the truncated mean down to 20 percent below it. The
notice's own worked example: a product measuring a mean SPF of 23 may print any
integer from 19 to 23. So the label is a conservative range, not a point
estimate, and two products showing the same figure are not necessarily the same
product.

Second, and more consequential: anything measuring 50 or above prints as SPF50+.
A product labelled SPF50+ might have measured 51. It might have measured 90.
There is no way to tell from the package, and comparing two SPF50+ products by
their labels is comparing two identical strings.

The UVA side is coarser again. PA is a four-step grade derived from the
ultraviolet-A protection factor, truncated to an integer, and shown only when
that integer reaches 2:

- PFA 2 to under 4: PA+, low
- PFA 4 to under 8: PA++, moderate
- PFA 8 to under 16: PA+++, high
- PFA 16 or above: PA++++, very high

The top band is open-ended, so PA++++ collapses everything from 16 upward into a
single symbol. And PA rests on a different photobiological endpoint from SPF --
persistent pigment darkening rather than erythema -- so the two are not
commensurable quantities and cannot be traded against each other.

Water resistance is not a marketing word here either. 내수성 or 지속내수성 may be
declared only when the water-resistance ratio confidence interval reaches 50
percent or more.

The measurement behind all of it: at least ten subjects per product, the 95
percent confidence interval on SPF required to fall within plus or minus 20
percent of the value, on PFA within plus or minus 17 percent, and re-testing with
a larger sample if those are not met. The notice also accepts foreign methods --
Japanese, US, European, Australian and New Zealand, or ISO 24444 -- as an
alternative basis for the SPF dossier.

One provision inverts the natural intuition and is worth knowing. A product with
SPF 10 or below is EXEMPT from filing the SPF, water-resistance and PA
substantiation data at all. The modest claim is not the better-evidenced claim
under this rule. It is the one with less evidence on file.

None of this touches what happens when the product meets a human being. That is
the applied-quantity problem, and it is a separate item.
