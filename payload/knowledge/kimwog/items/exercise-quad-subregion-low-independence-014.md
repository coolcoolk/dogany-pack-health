# exercise/quad-subregion-low-independence-014

Not every muscle splits cleanly into independently-trainable parts. A 7-week
study using only the back squat found that all four heads of the quadriceps
grew, but grew about the same amount as each other -- no statistically
significant difference between heads. That is a counterpoint to the general
idea that exercise selection can always carve out separate growth for
sub-parts of a muscle: for the quads under a compound squat, the parts seem
to move together.

This is one small study (n=18), so it should not overturn the general
regional-hypertrophy principle -- it just means the quadriceps-under-squat
case should be treated as a low-independence subregion split until better
evidence says otherwise, unlike the triceps long head or the biceps distal
case, which have their own dedicated longitudinal support.
