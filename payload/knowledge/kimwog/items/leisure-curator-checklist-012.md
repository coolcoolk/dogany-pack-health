# leisure/curator-checklist-012

How to grade a claim in this domain. Six gates, five general and one specific to
drinking.

**One. The exposure is a proxy for being well.** Nearly every leisure variable
requires health, money, mobility and other people to score highly on. So when an
effect looks too big for the activity that supposedly caused it, that is
information about the study design, not about the activity. Ask what would stop
an ill person from scoring high on this exposure, and discount by that.

**Two. Ask whose laboratory.** Researchers in this field usually like the thing
they study. The decisive result in the Mozart literature was not the size of the
effect but the fact that its size depended on which group measured it. Before
accepting a positive finding here, check whether the biggest numbers come from
the people who invented the claim.

**Three. Split by design quality before averaging.** The strongest thing this
domain holds was produced by sorting studies into those with random assignment
and active controls and those without, and showing the signal lived entirely in
the second pile. A meta-analysis that explains where the positive results came
from is worth more here than one that averages them away.

**Four. No far-transfer justification.** Music to intelligence, fiction to
empathy, arts to longevity -- every claim in this domain that a leisure activity
pays off somewhere else has either failed or shrunk to a laboratory task. Do not
recommend a hobby on the strength of a benefit outside the hobby. It is not
supported, and the person did not ask for a reason.

**Five. Self-reported hours are not a measurement.** The error is systematic, it
varies by person, and it bends conclusions rather than merely widening them.
Direction and change within one person are usable. Levels, comparisons between
people, and arithmetic reported back as fact are not.

**Six, drinking only. Check the reference group, and check the date.** An
apparent benefit at low intake is the calling card of a comparison group that
mixed lifelong abstainers with people who quit because they got ill. Any alcohol
source that predates the abstainer-bias and genetic-instrument corrections is
presumed to carry that flaw. Two things follow: prefer evidence built on gene
variants over evidence built on self-reported intake, because genotype is fixed
at conception and cannot be caused by illness; and disclose industry funding on
both sides of the argument, because this is the one lane here with an organised,
financed position.

**What to do with a claim that fails.** Refuse it and write down the refusal.
Do not drop it quietly. A silently dropped claim comes back next pass with nobody
left who remembers why it went. This domain's refusal list is longer than its
item list, which is the right shape for it, not a shortfall.

**And the tone.** Everything above is about not over-claiming benefits. None of
it is permission to turn somebody's free time into a health audit. Say what the
evidence says, decline to moralise, and keep in mind that the taxonomy this
domain was built from files drinking under hobbies -- not under risks.
