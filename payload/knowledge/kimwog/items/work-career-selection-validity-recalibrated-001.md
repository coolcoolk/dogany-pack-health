# work-career/selection-validity-recalibrated-001

What actually predicts how well someone will do a job was re-estimated in
2022, and most of the widely repeated numbers went down.

The argument is technical but the consequence is not. Studies of selection
methods are almost always run on people who were already hired, which is a
narrowed slice of the applicant pool, so the raw correlations are corrected
upward to estimate what they would have been in the full pool. The 2022
re-analysis argues the field built those corrections in ways that pushed the
estimates too high, and re-derives them.

The revised ordering, with the older figure first in each pair:

- structured employment interview: .51 to .42
- job knowledge test: .48 to .40
- empirically keyed biodata: .35 to .38 (the one that went up)
- work sample test: .54 to .33
- cognitive ability test: .51 to .31
- integrity test: .41 to .31
- assessment centre: .37 to .29
- conscientiousness: .31 to .19
- unstructured employment interview: .38 to .19
- years of prior work experience at the point of hire: .18 to .07 (the old
  figure measured tenure in the current job, a different question)

The ranking is the part worth carrying. The structured interview is now at
the top, well above the cognitive ability test. The two used to be tied at
.51, and most careers writing still treats cognitive ability as the stronger
predictor; the new gap is the reason a
recruiter who structures an interview is doing something measurably different
from one who does not.

Two honest caveats. The correction is disputed: a 2023 paper in the same
journal argues one of the key recommendations is unnecessary, and the original
authors replied in the same issue. And every one of these numbers is bounded
by how badly job performance itself is measured, which is a separate problem
carried by work-career/criterion-problem-supervisor-ratings-002.
