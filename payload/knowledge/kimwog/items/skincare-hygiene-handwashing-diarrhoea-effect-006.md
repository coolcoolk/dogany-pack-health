# skincare-hygiene/handwashing-diarrhoea-effect-006

Hand-washing is one of the few hygiene instructions with a real number attached,
and the number is about a third.

A Cochrane review pooled 29 randomised controlled trials: 13 in child-care
centres and schools in mainly high-income countries covering 54,471
participants, 15 community trials in low- and middle-income countries covering
29,347, and one hospital trial of 148 patients with AIDS.

- Child-care and schools, high-income countries: hand-washing promotion prevented
  about a third of diarrhoea episodes. Incidence rate ratio 0.70, interval 0.58
  to 0.85, nine trials, 4,664 participants, rated high certainty. This is the
  most solid single estimate in the review.
- Communities in low- and middle-income countries: about a quarter of episodes.
  Incidence rate ratio 0.71, interval 0.62 to 0.81, nine trials, 15,950
  participants, moderate certainty.

Two different settings, two different designs, convergent magnitude. That
convergence is what makes this usable rather than merely published.

The most interesting detail is in the community trials. Where the intervention
supplied free soap alongside education, the pooled risk ratio was 0.66. Where it
was education alone, it was 0.84 with an interval running to 1.05, which includes
no effect. Two trials against seven, so this is a direction rather than a
contrast test -- but the direction is that removing the material obstacle did the
work, not the telling.

The review's own limits are worth carrying, because they close off the
over-claims. Every trial and every follow-up measurement was short term, and the
authors say plainly that they provide no evidence about long-term effect. No
trial evaluated diarrhoea-related deaths. No trial evaluated cost-effectiveness.
Behaviour change itself was measured in only four trials with bias-prone methods.

Two boundaries. This item covers hand hygiene as a health behaviour, and stops at
the kitchen door: food handling, cutting-board contamination and Korean
food-safety instruction are owned by the cooking domain and answered from there,
not restated here. And it says nothing about respiratory infection, which is a
different and much more disputed literature that was not researched in this pass.
Its silence there is a not-yet-asked, not a clean bill of health.
