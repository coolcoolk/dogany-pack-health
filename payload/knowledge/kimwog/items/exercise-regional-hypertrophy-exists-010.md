# exercise/regional-hypertrophy-exists-010

Muscles do not grow uniformly along their length -- different parts of the
same muscle can grow by different amounts depending on which exercises are
used, and the reason is that different parts of a muscle get activated to
different degrees by different movements. The part that works hardest during
an exercise is the part that grows the most from it.

This finding is the foundation under any claim that a specific exercise
biases growth toward a specific sub-part of a muscle -- but it is a general
principle, not a certification of any one exercise-to-subregion pairing.
Individual exercise-bias claims (which movement favors which subregion) each
carry their own, separately assessed level of support.
