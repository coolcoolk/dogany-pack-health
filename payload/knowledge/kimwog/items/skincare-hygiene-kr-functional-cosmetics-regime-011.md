# skincare-hygiene/kr-functional-cosmetics-regime-011

Korea has a legal category for cosmetic claims that most English-language
skincare material has no equivalent for, and it decides where regulatory
scrutiny starts and stops on any Korean shelf.

Under the enforcement rule of the Cosmetics Act, 기능성화장품 covers exactly
eleven functions and nothing else:

1. preventing melanin deposition to suppress freckles and blemishes, aiding
   whitening
2. lightening melanin already deposited, aiding whitening
3. giving elasticity to alleviate or improve wrinkles
4. protecting the skin while tanning it evenly under strong sun
5. blocking or scattering ultraviolet radiation to protect the skin
6. changing hair colour, including bleaching and decolouring, excluding
   temporary colour
7. removing body hair, excluding physical removal
8. aiding alleviation of hair-loss symptoms, excluding products that merely coat
   the hair to look thicker
9. aiding alleviation of acne-prone skin -- limited to cleansing products
10. restoring skin-barrier function, the rule defining the barrier as the
    epidermis of the outermost stratum corneum, to aid improvement of itching
11. aiding the lightening of red stretch-mark lines

Before any of those claims can be sold, the manufacturer, responsible
distributor or research institution must clear a gate: either a review by, or a
filed report to, the head of the National Institute of Food and Drug Safety
Evaluation. Registered products carry the 기능성화장품 mark on the package, whose
design is prescribed in the packaging annex.

Two readings of this, and only one of them is right.

The wrong one is that the mark means the product works. It does not. It means
the product went through the review-or-report gate for one of eleven enumerated
functions. There is no effect size attached to it and no independent body
concluded anything about how well it performs.

The right one is a boundary marker. The eleven functions are a much shorter list
than the marketing vocabulary. Hydration. Pore size. Barrier repair as a beauty
claim. Microbiome balance. Firming. Lifting. None of those is a registered
functional claim under Korean law. A product asserting them is making an ordinary
cosmetic claim with no registration behind it at all, and the difference is
visible on the package.

One discipline to carry forward. Every instrument here is recent: the enforcement
rule took effect in April 2026, both ministry notices in December 2025, the
packaging annex was amended in August 2025. Korean cosmetic regulation revises
fast. Check the edition before quoting a provision.

And one thing deliberately absent. How this compares to the American structure is
the obvious next question and it is not answered here, because direct retrieval
of the US regulator's own pages failed in this pass. This lane does not state one
country's rules from memory while citing another's from the statute.
