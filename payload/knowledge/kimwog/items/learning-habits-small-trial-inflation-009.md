# learning-habits/small-trial-inflation-009

Small trials systematically inflate reported effect sizes for behavioral
interventions: studies with 100 or fewer participants averaged more than
triple the effect size of studies with over 370 participants. Restricting a
literature to the largest trials only flips more than a quarter of previously
"significant" results to non-significant. The typical correction to any one
meta-analysis is smaller -- a median shift of about 0.05 standardised units --
so the rule is a sensitivity check, not a fixed discount.

Rule: before assigning a top-tier grade to a behavioral meta-analysis, check
whether the result is sensitive to trial size. This specific estimate was
derived from childhood-obesity intervention trials and is generalized here to
behavioral interventions broadly -- carry that as an applicability caveat.
