# home/diy-disinfectant-efficacy-012

Vinegar and baking soda do not disinfect. That is the claim, and it is worth
being precise about what it does and does not cover, because the imprecision is
where the argument usually happens.

An infection-control group ran a quantitative suspension test comparing
household natural products against a set of commercial disinfectants, at thirty
seconds and at five minutes of contact. Vinegar eliminated less than three
log10 of Staphylococcus aureus and Escherichia coli. Baking soda eliminated less
than three log10 of every organism tested. The commercial disinfectants
completely inactivated both antibiotic-resistant and susceptible bacteria at
both exposure times. Against poliovirus, only two of the tested products worked,
and the natural products were not among them.

That is a wide margin, not a close call.

Now the precision. This is a finding about disinfection -- killing organisms. It
is not a finding about cleaning. Removing grease, dissolving limescale,
absorbing odour, lifting soil physically off a surface: those are real jobs,
they are useful, and they are not what was tested. Vinegar remains good at the
things vinegar is good at.

What is refused is the substitution. If the reason you are wiping something is
that you want the organisms on it dead, a home-made vinegar spray is not doing
that job, and believing it is doing that job is worse than not wiping at all,
because it ends the process.

Three honest caveats.

The study is from 2000. Twenty-six years is a long time to lean on one paper. It
is carried because the measurement is direct and the failure margin is enormous,
not because anyone rechecked recently. Dilute acetic acid at household strength
has not become a different chemical, but a modern replication would still be
worth having and none was found in this pass.

It is a suspension test, not a surface test. Organisms were challenged in
liquid, not on a greasy worktop. That matters, and it matters in the direction
that makes the result stronger, not weaker -- organic soil interferes with
disinfectants, so real surfaces are harder than suspension, and the natural
products already failed on the easy version.

And the vinegar-plus-baking-soda combination that circulates as a cleaning hack
is not covered here in either direction, because no primary measurement of it
was located at all. Nothing to endorse, nothing to debunk. One thing is not
optional though: none of this loosens item 008. Bleach does not get combined
with anything, vinegar very much included.
