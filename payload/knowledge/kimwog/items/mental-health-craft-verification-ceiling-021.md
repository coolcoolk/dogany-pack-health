# mental-health/craft-verification-ceiling-021

This lane holds one kind of knowledge: how an assistant should conduct itself
around things it can see about a person's life. Not what is true about people --
that is the adjacent lane, and it is graded against trials and meta-analyses.
This one is graded against practice, which in this case means practice inside a
single estate, unblinded, with no control group and no measured outcome. That is
an honest n of one. It is the ceiling of this lane and it does not move.

So why open it at all.

Because the evidence lane answers a question nobody actually asks in the
moment. It says what is established about sleep and mood, about workload and
exhaustion, about what a questionnaire score does and does not mean. What it
does not say is what an assistant should do at nine in the morning when the
calendar has been heavy for three weeks and the person has not mentioned it.
That decision gets made either way. Written down and capped, it can at least be
argued with. Left unwritten it gets improvised, and improvised conduct in this
exact area has already produced a documented failure in this estate: an
automated check read a missing record as a missed commitment and pushed the
accusation at the owner, who was able to correct it only because he happened to
remember otherwise.

Now the part that matters more than the contents.

A craft lane opened next to an evidence lane that has just refused a pile of
popular material is a door. The evidence lane looked at gratitude journalling
and found that its effect against an active comparison is close to nothing. It
looked at positive-psychology exercises and found the headline numbers collapse
under correction for small-sample bias. It looked at willpower as a depletable
resource and found two large preregistered multi-laboratory replications that
found nothing. It looked at inferring mood from tracked behaviour and found the
field's own systematic reviewers capping their results at hypothesis generation.

Every one of those is now sitting outside a door marked "practitioner
knowledge", and the argument for letting them back through is always the same
one: it is not a claim, it is just something an assistant could helpfully do.
That argument is refused. Dropping the citation from a claim does not make it a
softer claim. It makes it an unsourced one. All four stay refused in this lane,
at full strength.

The admission test, then, is three conditions and an item needs all three.

It has to be about the assistant's conduct, not the person's condition. "How to
say something without asserting a state" belongs here. "How to tell whether
someone is struggling" does not, at any wording.

It has to be falsifiable by watching the assistant fail, not by measuring a
human outcome. If checking whether the rule is right would require a trial, then
the rule is an evidence claim wearing craft clothes, and it goes to the other
lane or nowhere.

And it has to not be something the evidence lane already turned away.

The clinical boundary comes across unchanged, because a change of lane is not a
change of scope. No diagnosis. No screening. No risk assessment. No crisis
handling. No treatment selection. And the rule that governs every item in here:
no score, no threshold, no tracked signal becomes a statement about what a
person is or has. This lane can describe how to speak carefully in the presence
of an observation. It can never describe how to draw a conclusion about a person
from one.

Where a topic can only be said usefully as clinical advice, the answer is not to
write a gentler version of it. It is to write it down in the gap file as
out-of-scope by design, and leave the space empty on purpose, so that the next
person to look does not mistake a decision for an oversight.
